                         Why Study Combustion?
    • Energy
            – While we might associate fire with the Stone Age, we still make
              extensive use of combustion in our daily lives.
                   • Electricity – about 70% in the U.S. from combustion
                   • Heating of buildings, water, food, and in manufacturing processes
                   • Transportation
            – Our reliance on imported fuel contributes to our national trade
              deficit and affects our national security.
    • Environment
            – Combustion is a major source of greenhouse gases.
            – Soot contributes to global warming and is a health problem.
    • Fire Safety

    Given its pervasive use with annual U.S. fuel costs on the order
    of a trillion dollars even small improvements in combustion
    technology can significantly reduce fuel needs and
    pollution production!

              properties of flames can be used to characterize (by        0g, -0.6 kV, gas-jet

              chemical or local convective influences).

              combustion performance

                                        Burners & Igniter
  • Four types of burners are planned:
         • spherical burners                    0.25, 0.375, 0.5 inch diameter, porous sintered metal
         • gas-jet burners                      0.4, 0.8, 1.3, 1.6, 2.1, 2.7 and 3.2 mm ID
         • coflow burner                        2.1-mm ID inner tube, 25-mm ID outer tube
         • flat-plate burners                   2.5, 5 cm diameter, porous (with heat flux transducers)
  • Thermocouple for surface temperature on most burners
  • Burners will be electrically isolated
  • Hot-wire igniter, retracting, similar to that in use for MDCA
         • Igniter tips are replaceable, e.g., to match the burner type/size

                         Nominal Operational Sequence
    • Flow > Ignite > Measure > Extinguish
            – Initiate burner flows at ignition condition
            – Ignite flame
                   • Wait for flame to stabilize
            – If needed, adjust flows to initial test condition
                   • Wait for flame to stabilize
            – If desired:
                   • increase or decrease burner flow(s)
                         – to vary flow/velocity or concentration
                   • activate and adjust electric field
                   • translate TFP array
            – Stop burner flows
                   • per extinction detection, test duration, etc.

    • Control can be automated or “manual”
