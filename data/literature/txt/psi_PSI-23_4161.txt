      ZIN Technologies                                   ACME Critical Design Review– 150
                                    Depress / Repress
Glenn Research Center



  • Unless part of a pressurized system, all volumes are designed to
    adequately vent due to station / vehicle depressurization
  • Vented Containers will be verified by the stored energy method (SDP
    STD-ACME-1 17.a) where the volume has a stored energy < 3,063 ft-
    lbs. Volumes with a stored energy > 3,063 ft-lbs will have a depress
    rate calculated.

  • Depress rates are calculated* for each volume to show that the
    volume’s depress rate is greater than maximum station / vehicle
    depress rate so no pressure load is generated
  • Maximum depress rate is defined in section 3.1.1.2.1.5 part B of SSP-
    50835 Rev C as 13.3kPa/s (1.93psi/sec)
     *AIAA Journal, October 1986, Vol. 24, No. 10, pg 1709-1711, “Formulas for Venting or Charging
     Gas from a Single Volume”, H.T. Yang

      Title: Exposure of Crew to Toxic Byproducts During Chamber Operations
      Description: Carcinogenic soot particles from the combustion chamber
      contaminate the habitable atmosphere causing crew illness if inhaled or
      floating particles could cause injury to the eyes.
      Controls:
      1. The soot production for ACME experiments will be below the SMAC limit
            and does not exceed Tox 0.
      2. The chamber air will be circulated through a tested Adsorber Cartridge
            for 15 minutes until free floating soot is removed, prior to venting.
      3. The chemicals selected for the Adsorber Cartridge will remove the soot
            and combustion by-products.
      4. Each Adsorber Cartridge shall have a content label and serial number.
      5. Approved crew procedures shall be used for installation and removal of
            an Adsorber Cartridge. (TBR) (This control is also covered in CIR Hazard

  Title: Adiabatic Combustion Ruptures CIR Combustion Chamber or CIR Exhaust
  Vent Package during ACME experiments (except for the Inverse Flame Experiment)
  Description: Rapid overpressurization during the combustion of ACME gaseous
  fuel and oxidizer/diluent exceeds the MDP of the CIR Combustion chamber and/or
  exhaust vent package resulting in damage to CIR or ISS hardware or injury to the
  crew.
  Note (on hazard report):
  1. This hazard report works in conjunction with CIR hazard report FCF-C07, “Adiabatic Combustion
     ruptures the chamber walls, causing damage to station and/or injury to crew.“ Overpressurization due
     to combustion will be controlled by limiting the fuel volume in each gas bottle. The CIR Valve Timers
     will be placed in the fuel mode. The chamber will be pre-filled with gaseous oxidizer and gaseous fuel
     will flow through the ACME burner.
  2. CIR is responsible for filling the experiment gas bottles. The ACME detonation analyses determine the
     limits on fuel volumes in each bottle. The fuel gas volumes will be chosen such that even if the entire
     contents of a bottle were to be released into the CIR Combustion Chamber, any adiabatic combustion
     would result in a pressure less than the MDP of the CIR Combustion chamber. The MDP of the CIR
     chamber is 135 psia.
  3. The CIR bottle leakage hazard controls and verifications are covered in CIR hazard report FCF-C08
     Leakage of Pressurized System.
  4. The CIR system rupture hazard controls and verifications are covered in CIR hazard report FCF-C10
     Rupture of Pressurized Systems.
