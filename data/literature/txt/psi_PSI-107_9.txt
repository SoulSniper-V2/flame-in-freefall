                                Introduction

•   Three investigations will be performed: SPICE, SLICE, and BASS.
•   These investigations will be performed inside the Microgravity Science
    Glovebox (MSG).
•   SPICE is based on the Enclosed Laminar Flames (ELF) hardware which
    flew on STS-87 in 1997.
•   SPICE hardware is already on-orbit. It has successfully completed all of its
    objectives with the collection of 200 data points in the first half of 2009. New
    fuel is being flown on ULF5 to support the SLICE and BASS experiments.
•   SPICE hardware will also be used to perform SLICE and BASS.
•   SPICE burns different fuels to investigate the point at which they begin to
    smoke.
•   SLICE burns different fuels to investigate the structure of flames when lifted
    from the fuel nozzle.
•   BASS burns different materials to investigate how they burn in microgravity.

                  SLICE Science Background

•   The goal of the SLICE investigation is to investigate the structure of lifting
    and lifted flames; whereby the flame detaches from the nozzle and
    stabilizes at a downstream position.
•   The results of the SLICE experiment will be used to minimize risk and
    maximize the scientific return of the CLD (Coflow Laminar Diffusion)
    Flame experiment, which is one of the four ACME (Advanced Combustion
    via Microgravity Experiments) investigations that are currently in
    development for conduct within the Combustion Integrated Rack (CIR).

                    BASS Science Background
•   The BASS investigation tests the hypothesis that materials in microgravity burn as well
    if not better than the same material in normal gravity with other conditions being
    identical (ie. pressure, oxygen concentration, temperature).
•   To test this hypothesis with any but the thinnest materials requires extended
    microgravity time – longer than available in drop towers or parabolic aircraft. If this
    hypothesis is true, it implies serious safety concerns over the utility and margin of
    safety provided NASA-STD-6001 Test #1 (upward flammability test used to evaluate
    materials intended for use in the habitable environments of US spacecraft). Practical,
    realistic (thicker) fuels in typical geometries will be examined, including wake flames
    which have characteristics of a fire burning behind another object (for example, a fire
    burning in tight area which may not be easily accessible).
•   There are important differences in the suppression of fires in space compared to on
    Earth. On Earth it is understood that best results are generally obtained when the
    extinguisher “attacks” the base of the flame, which is both the stabilization point and
    the point of fresh air entrainment. For a fire burning in microgravity, the best point of
    application of suppressant may not be immediately apparent, especially for a partially
    obstructed flame or a wake-stabilized flame. Depending on the geometry of the flame
    and the characteristics of the extinguisher (distance from flame, dispersion angle) it is
    possible that the suppressant stream will be ineffective or might actually make the
    flame worse through the entrainment of oxygen.

                            Earth Applications
•   SPICE
    The smoke-point phenomena is a classical metric in the understanding of the heat
    release and spread rate of fires. It is commonly used in fire modeling on Earth and to
    understand the soot growth and emission by flames. The dominant characteristics of
    many flames of practical interest are nonbuoyant. SPICE seeks to extend our
    understanding by looking at the interaction of ambient flow with the smoke point,
    enabling us to better predict heat release from non-buoyant flames in practical
    combustors (e.g. jet engines and furnaces).
•   SLICE
    The twin purposes for the SLICE research are increased efficiency and reduced
    pollutant emission in practical combustion devices. More explicitly, the experiment is
    being conducted to advance combustion modeling capability, which “allows system
    designers to improve efficiency and reduce harmful pollutants in ways never before
    possible” (as stated by an external reviewer of the SLICE experiment). In other
    words, the SLICE research is being conducted to advance energy technology and
    improve our global environment.
•   BASS
    It is highly desirable to develop a new and/or improved Earth-based test method for
    material flammability. The data obtained in this experiment will begin to bridge the
    gap between the normal gravity method, ground-based microgravity tests, and actual
    material flammability in microgravity

            Major Unique Hazard Controls
• After every 2 burns the MSG Work Volume must be purged for 3
  minutes to promote air mixing to dilute any HF concentration
  byproduct produced during combustion.
   – MSG Glove Port open
   – MSG Air Cir ON
• Each Gas Bottle Assembly must have a tie wrap valve restraint
  in place before stowing.
• Prior to crew access into MSG, the Gas Bottle Assembly valve
  must be closed.
• When the MSG Work Volume is open and a Gas Bottle
  Assembly is present, MSG Air Cir must be ON.
• The inside of the Experiment Assembly must be permitted to
  cool to a temp below 49°C before crew access is permitted.
   – Wait time for SLICE is 1 minute (covered by 3 minute purge)
   – Wait time for BASS thin and candle samples is 2 minutes (covered by 3
     minute purge)
   – Wait time for BASS sphere and thick samples is 9 minutes
