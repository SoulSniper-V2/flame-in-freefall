Purpose:
The purpose of this report is to document the results of a study conducted in search of the
ideal type of wire to be used for the hot wire ignitors for the Smoke Experiment.

Objective:
The objective of this study was to find a wire with a consistent, linear resistance versus
temperature characteristic and with a “significant” slope.

Scope:
This study was conducted in the Phase A Laboratory (Bldg 77, Room 154A) on
commercially available wire. The ignitor manufacturing technique and the pyrolization
of samples is beyond the scope of this study.

Procedure:
Each type of wire was mounted on a test fixture. The test fixture was then placed into a
ThermCraft laboratory oven. The oven was programmed to ramp up to a set point, hold
the set point for fifteen minutes, and then ramp to the next set point until the final
temperature of approximately 800 oC was attained. (See oven cycle graph in Appendix
C.) Resistance readings were taken near the end of each “dwell” period. Several of the
wire types were subjected to additional tests to examine the effects of the previous
thermal cycle(s).

The test fixture held approximately thirty-four inches (0.86 meters) of wire.

A stainless steel sheathed type-k thermocouple (Omega Part No. KQSS-116U-24) was
placed inside the oven in close proximity to the wire under test.

Leads of high temperature furnace wire (#18 AWG) were brought out of the oven to
enable the test conductor to measure the resistance of the wire under test.
The resistance of the leads and the terminations was characterized by subjection the
fixture and leads to a thermal cycle with a copper bus bar shorting the terminals of the
fixture.

The fixture resistance was subtracted from the total resistance reading for each test point
to determine the net resistance of the wire under test.

The results were recorded in an Excel Workbook. Several different data plots were
prepared and studied.

Summary and Conclusions:
This study examined the characteristics of eight types of wire: anodized mild steel, pure
iron, Alomega (similar to Alumel), -Stainless steel, pure platinum, platinum-rhodium,
tungster-5%rhenium, and tungsten-26%rhenium. Traditional heating wires, Nichrome
and Kanthal were also included for comparison.

Pure platinum has close-to-ideal characteristics. However the cost of the material may be
prohibitive.

Alomega and stainless steel ran a close second.

Mild steel, iron and tungsten-composites oxidized in the oven and their characteristics
changed significantly from one thermal cycle to the next.

Nichrome and Kanthal have too flat a slope to meet the objectives.

Further testing will be conducted to determine the optimum wire type and wire diameter.
Representative test samples will by pyrolized using various ignitor configurations. The
quality and quantity of the smoke will be visually evaluated.

Test Results:
The tests were conducted in the Phase A Lab from February 6, 2002 through March 20
2002, by Len Miller and Nathan Funk of Zin Technologies, Inc.

The individual results for each test were tabulated and plotted. For those wires subjected
to more than one thermal cycle, the comparative results were plotted. Finally, an overall
plot of the combined results was prepared.

The entire Excel Workbook, with tabulated test results and plots thereof, is included in
Appendix A of this report.

Photographs of the laboratory apparatus are in Appendix B and a graph of the thermal
cycle is in Appendix C.
