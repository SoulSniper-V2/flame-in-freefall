1 INTRODUCTION

The Smoke Point in Co-flow Experiment (SPICE) Investigation will be operated onboard the
International Space Station (ISS) in the Microgravity Science Glovebox (MSG). A previous
version of the SPICE design was called the Enclosed Laminar Flames (ELF) experiment, which
flew in the Middeck Glovebox on the Space Shuttle STS-87. The Enclosed Laminar Flames
(ELF) experiment objective was to study fundamental phenomena related to the mechanisms
controlling the stability and extinction of jet diffusion flames. The design modifications made as
a result of a carrier change (Middeck GBX to ISS MSG) are considered significant enough that
the SPICE Hardware is not considered Series Flight Hardware of the original ELF design.

This Phase III Flight Safety Data Package (SDP) documents the hazards assessed for SPICE.
This SDP documents compliance to NSTS/ISS 1700.7B and is prepared in accordance with
NSTS/ISS 13830C. A brief overview of the experiment hardware and MSG interfaces is
presented first, followed by a brief description of crew procedures. Finally, a safety assessment
of SPICE Hazards is presented. Hazards that have not been eliminated by design have been
addressed and documented in the Standard Hazard Form (JSC 1230 Form) or in a Unique
Hazard Report (JSC 542B Form). Refer to Section 4 of this document for the SPICE Safety
Assessment. Refer to Attachment B for the SPICE Standard Hazard Reporting Form, (JSC
1230 Form). Refer to Attachment C for the SPICE Unique Hazard Reporting Forms, (JSC 542B)
that support the SPICE safety assessment.

1.3 EXPERIMENT OVERVIEW
The predecessor investigation to Smoke Point in Co-flow Experiment (SPICE) was called
Enclosed Laminar Flames (ELF) and was developed by NASA/GRC for flight on the Shuttle
Spacelab during the Fourth United States Microgravity Payload (USMP-4 / STS-87) mission in
November 1997.

SPICE continues to study fundamental phenomena related to understanding the mechanisms
controlling the stability and extinction of jet diffusion flames. SPICE will stabilize an enclosed
laminar flame, measure the overall flame shape to validate the theoretical and numerical
predictions, measure the flame stabilization heights, and measure the temperature field to verify
flame structure predictions.

The differences in ELF and SPICE hardware are the following:

       The Gas Bottle Assembly size increased from 75 ml to 150 ml, and maximum pressure
was reduced from 350 psi to 230 psi. ELF bottles were open on both ends with a plug placed in
one end and the valve in the other. For SPICE only one end of the bottle has an opening.

        The original fuel was 50/50 % methane. Propane, Ethylene, Propylene have been added
as fuels. The increase in bottle size and decrease in pressure cause the worst case SPICE
bottle to have three times the energy of an ELF bottle due to fuel. Also the number of bottles
increased from 6 to 12.

        For the Experiment Assembly (EA) the Mass Flow Controller model was changed.

      There is a new Electronics power video box power box, a new video camera, a new still
camera and there is an interface with MSG video drawer.


1.4 PAYLOAD AND MISSION OVERVIEW
The SPICE payload is currently scheduled to fly on the International Space Station (ISS) in the
Microgravity Science Glovebox (MSG). The ascent flight on the NSTS is tentatively scheduled
to be ULF2. All SPICE hardware will be returned to earth at the same time on a future shuttle
flight.



1.4.1 SPICE Payload Hardware

SPICE payload hardware is all new hardware that has been developed from the ELF
experiment hardware design.

SPICE consists of an Experiment Assembly (EA), 12 Gas Bottle Assemblies, a Nikon camera, a
Video Camera, associated structural support for the cameras, Video Power Unit, and a number
of stowage cases for the hardware and harnessing which includes a Control Box. SPICE is
providing two bottle stowage cases, a component stowage case, and 2 tape bags. The rest of
the hardware will be packed by JSC/MSFC. See figures 1 and 2 for hardware and figure 9b for a
case diagram respectively.

1.5.5 MSG Thermal Interfaces
Although the SPICE Experiment Assembly is mounted to the MSG Cold Plate. Cooling service
provided by the Cold Plate is not required for SPICE operations. The heat dissipated by the
burning process will raise the temperature of the air flowing through the Experiment Assembly
only a few degrees. The heat will be then dissipated to the surroundings.
1.5.6 MSG Working Volume and Air Filtration System
The SPICE experiment combustion process is run with the MSG Working Volume configured in
the normal mode (MSG Fans on lowest setting) with the side loading ports and glove ports
installed. This mode provides one level of containment for combustion by-products (all by-
products are tox level 0). At the end of the combustion process the MSG fans maybe changed
to a higher speed and run for a 5-minute period in order to re-circulate the Working Volume air
and clean-up combustion byproducts using the Working Volume Air Filtration System.
1.5.7 Crew Interfaces
The crew interfaces with SPICE consist of set up, nozzle change out, video tape change out,
IBM micro drive change out, fuel bottle change out, ignition, smoke point setting, still camera
shots, and tear down. Section 3.0 describes the operations in more detail.

2.1 EXPERIMENT ASSEMBLY
The Experiment Assembly (see Figure 4) is composed of an aluminum rectangular chamber
34.6cm X 17.0cm X 26.2cm with a ¼ inch polycarbonate Lexan 9034 window cover and the
associated stainless steel plumbing system that regulates the flow of gas. A commercial three
(3) inch diameter fan at the inlet to the chamber provides the air Co-flow. The fans are Panaflo
FBA08A12HIA axial fans with a nominal speed of 2950 RPM with plastic housing and blades.
The fan is placed in an aluminum housing and metal screens are located at the inlet and exit of
the fan, and at the exhaust end of the chamber. A ceramic (Cordierite) flow straightener is also
located in the fan housing. It is contained in a housing with a 100 mesh screen over it. A heat
sink/hand guard is also placed at the exhaust end of the Experiment Assembly. The heat sink is
a piece of copper with holes in it. It is contained in an aluminum box with corrugated bronze
mesh over the opening. The experiment Assembly is also equipped with a data logger and
PCMCIA (data card type of digital storage) storage. The Experiment Assembly Cover opens to
facilitate Nozzle and Igniter hardware installation and change-out operations. The thermal mass
of the Experiment Assembly precludes the potential to exceed touch temperature limits.

The Experiment Assembly also includes the Fuel Delivery System (See Figure 4A), which
consists of stainless steel assemblies and component parts such as the Fuel Bottle Assembly
(1-7), a male half Quick Disconnect (8), Filter (10), Pressure Regulator (13), Mass Flow
Controller (15), and applicable stainless steel tubing and adapter fittings (9,11,12,14,16). See
table 1 attached to Hazard Report SPICE-U2 for the Maximum Design Pressures (MDP) and
other information concerning these components. .

2.2 VIDEO TITLER BOX
The Video Titler Box is mounted on an aluminum adapter plate along with the Power
Conditioning Box (see Section 2.3). The complete assembly is called the Power-Video Unit (See
Figure 5).

This Video Titler Box receives camera video and superimposes SPICE text messages on it.
Experiment data is presented on the video for tape recording and for display to crewmembers.

The Video Titler Box is an aluminum enclosure approximately 8 in. x 5.5 in. x 3 in. The Video
Titler Box has six (6) connections: Power from Power Conditioning Box, Control (RS-232), two
(2) video inputs, one each from the SPICE Video camera and D100 Digital camera and two (2)
video outputs which are connected to the MSG Video Drawer via a MSG Working Volume front
feed-through connector.

Internal to the Video Titler Box are three (3) conformal coated printed circuit boards and
associated wiring. The circuit receives the two (2) video signals from the SPICE Video camera
and Digital Camera adds text and outputs the appropriate video format to the MSG Video
Drawer. The MSG Video Drawer contains the video monitoring equipment that will recorder the
SPICE video signals received onto SPICE provided digital video tapes.

Video Titler Box power is provided from the SPICE Power Conditioning Box (see section 2.3).
Control of the Video Titler Box is via (RS-232) link to the SPICE Experiment Assembly.




2.3 POWER CONDITIONING BOX
This box is located in the Power/Video Assembly and is attached to the adapter plate. It
provides SPICE with power conditioning elements required for proper SPICE operation with the
MSG. These include wire gauge/routing sites and additional circuit protection Fuses and spares
(see Figure 5). Two captive Thumb Screws secure the lid of the box. Backing off the screws and
removing the lid accomplish access to the fuses. Fuses can be replaced within the Power
Conditioning Box without having to remove the Box from MSG.

Power conditioning elements are housed in an aluminum enclosure (with lid) very similar to the
Video Titler Box. The Power Conditioning Box includes a power input cable that is connected to
the MSG Working Volume J303 Power Connector. A terminal board inside the Power
Conditioning Box provides power distribution for +/-12, +5, and +28 VDC power provided from
MSG. The Power Conditioning Box also contains a 28VDC to 9VDC Converter and Filter that
provides 9VDC power to the NIKON D100 Camera. The filter, capacitors, and a resistor are to
limit the inrush current when the camera snaps a picture.

Two (2) 5 Amp Cartridge Fuses with one wired to the +5 VDC power and the other wired to +28
VDC power are also located inside the Power Conditioning Box. The fuses provide additional
circuit protection to the SPICE Experiment Hardware that is wired to the +5 and +28 VDC
power. The SPICE Experiment Hardware that uses +/- 12VDC power does not require
additional circuit protection.

2.9 CABLE ASSEMBLIES
SPICE has the following cable assemblies, Power Cable Assembly, Video Cable assembly, Still
Camera Cable Assembly, and Feed-through Cable Assembly. These are all stowed in the
SPICE Component Stowage Case. The wires are Teflon wires rated for 200 C and are bundled
together with expandable cable mesh. See SPICE Wire Derating Analysis 60054-ANA-017.

The Power Cable Assembly consists of the cables which run from the Power/Video Unit to the
Experiment Assembly, to the Video Camera, and to the Still Camera. This cable supplies power
to these units.

The Video Cable assembly is a video data cable which connects the Video Camera to the
Power/Video Unit for signals.

The Still Camera Cable Assembly. This cable connects the Power/Video unit to the Still Camera
for signal.

The Feed-through Cable Assembly has 4 ends and is used for signals except for one 12 volt
wire to the experiment assembly. One end connects to the MSG feed through connector to
provide communication to the SPICE Control Box. The control box itself has a cable and
connector which is permanently attached to the Control Box. The control box consists of two 10
turn pots, three toggle switches, a feed through, a multi-positional switch which switches
resistors to set the radiometer gain. Another end of the cable connects to the Still Camera to
snap pictures. Another end connects to the Experiment Assembly to start data collection,
enable fuel flow, set the radiometer, set the fuel flow, set the fan flow, and enable the ignitor.
The fourth end connects to the Power/Video Unit.


2.10 SOFTWARE SUMMARY

Data gathering software resides in the Experiment Assembly’s Data Logger. The Control Box
Assembly is equipped with switches for controlling the fan speed and fuel flow rate, and
activates the Ignitor and data acquisition. Video images are taken with a Video Camera. The
video data is recorded on a MSG provided video recorder. Digital pictures are also taken of the
top view through the Experiment Assemblies Lexan window cover. The video images, together
with the digital data output of the data logger, are sent to a video box, which writes the digital
data (air flow velocity, fuel flow, fan voltage, and time) on the video signal. The video signal
from this box is then recorded by the MSG video recorder and down linked.
The SPICE experiment will fail-safe in the event of total or partial power loss, i.e., the hardware
will not be damaged or pose any hazard in the event of a power disruption. In the event of a
total power loss, the SPICE experiment cannot be operated and the normally closed MFC will
shut, cutting off fuel flow. Experiment sequencing is controlled by the crew. Software is only
used for data recording in the SPICE Experiment.

4.2.1 Structural Failure

SPICE hardware is designed to meet the expected launch, on-orbit and return loads specified in
MSFC-RQMT-2888, Paragraph 3.1.1.1, and Table 1 which is consistent with SSP 57000
requirements. The stowage loads in MSFC-RQMT-2888 are based on a worst case condition of
the Middeck and MPLM stowage loads. SPICE hardware will be stowed in soft transfer bags
and transported in the MPLM for transfer to the ISS. All the SPICE hardware will be soft stowed
for return. ZIN Tech Engineering has performed an analysis of test levels specified in NASA-
STD-7001 (NASA Payload Vibration Test Criteria) and MSFC-RQMT-2888 to define vibration
test levels to encompass the launch and landing load levels the hardware will be exposed to
and has created a composite vibration loading profile to envelope the levels defined in both
MSFC-RQMT-2888 and NASA-STD-7001. The SPICE flight hardware has undergone
acceptance vibration testing at these levels. A test report has been prepared to confirm that the
test was successfully completed.

The SPICE hardware is also designed to meet the expected on-orbit loads during operation in
the MSG. SPICE hardware will be fastened to the MSG working volume using captive,
threaded fasteners. This will meet the on orbit load requirements as specified in SSP 57000 and
MSFC-RQMT-2888. The hardware inside the MSG will not be exposed to on-orbit kick loads
during operation. The control box is subjected to crew induced loads and is capable of
withstanding the loads per SSP 57000 and MSFC-RQMT-2888.

The Gas Bottle Assembly Stowage Case and the SPICE component cases have two latches
which keep them from opening in stowage. The case itself is packed in foam and placed in the
stowage volume which will prevent the case from opening during launch, therefore they are not
safety critical.

SPICE structural failure is addressed in Section 1 of STD-SPICE-01.

4.2.5 Shatterable Material Release

Use of frangible material is limited to the fuses, flow straightener, Still Camera lens and Video
Camera lens. The SPICE Experiment Assembly Window cover is made out of Polycarbonate
Lexan 9034 and is not shatterable.

The camera lenses will be launched with protective covers on since the glass of the Still and
Video Camera lens are recessed in their respective housings. During SPICE set-up Crew
procedure the cameras will be installed into the MSG Working Volume before the protective
covers are removed from the camera lens. The crew will assess/inspect the lens for damage at
the time of removal. Once the caps are removed and assessment is acceptable the MSG
Working Volume enclosure will then provide containment when the protective covers have been
removed from the cameras during experiment operations. These items have also been
vibration tested and inspected.

The flow straightener is made of Cordierite which is a ceramic and is contained inside the fan
housing on the Experiment Assembly (EA). Similar to acceptable controls for optical glass, the
EA is also packaged in a clear plastic bag and inspected by the crew after launch. If it were to
break, the pieces could be seen through the window of the EA. The EA is inside MSG when
removed from the bag and when operating. If any particles are fine enough to get through the
screens they will be ingested in the MSG HEPA filters. Since intake and outlets are screened
and inside EA there are no identifiable failure modes on orbit to damage the ceramic. The 3 X 3
X 1 inch piece of Cordierite is also bound along the 3 X 3 perimeter which will prevent damage
and break up of this honey comb type material. Similar to control of optical glass such as lenses
(form 1230 section 5) the item is vibration tested and inspected for damage afterwards. See
figures 13a and 13b.

The fuses in the Power Box are also glass. They are wrapped in Kapton tape, which is never
removed, to contain any breakage that might occur. They are contained inside the Power
Conditioning Box. The crew does not have access to the interior of the Power Conditioning
Box.

4.2.10      Touch Temperatures

SPICE hardware and electronics have been designed and tested to meet touch temperature
limits of 49°C specified in the Interpretation Letter MA2-95-048. Also during on-orbit operation,
the SPICE hardware is enclosed inside the MSG working volume with no direct crew contact
with the SPICE Experiment Assembly during ignition or combustion processes (experiment
runs). Also during post experiment runs the airflow provided by the MSG Air Handling Unit
(AHU) during by-product clean up will also aid in the dissipation of any residual heated air within
the MSG working volume. SPICE by design does not require external cooling during experiment
operations.

A thermal test has been conducted to verify that the touch temperature limits of MA2-95-048 are
met. The fuel nozzles were tested and found that the worst case nozzle reached 89 degrees C
but cooled to 33 degrees C in less than 60 seconds after flame off. This was without EA air flow
which will be present at the end of a burn decreasing cooling time. A wait time will be imposed
before EA chamber access after flame off.

Touch Temperature is addressed in hazard report U6.

4.3.1 Pressurized Systems

SPICE pressurized components are the Gas Bottle Assemblies and the Experiment Assembly
Fuel Delivery System. These components do not interface with the nitrogen or vacuum sources
of the MSG.

Even though all of the components are rated for pressures well above 230 psi, the system MDP
is determined by the point at which the fuel can turn to liquid. This pressure also considered the
maximum temperature that the bottle could experience. SPICE pressurized lines and fittings will
be designed to a factor of safety of 4.0 X System MDP of 230psid. SPICE Pressurized system
components will be designed to a factor of safety of greater than or equal to 2.5 X System MDP

May 2008
                                                39
SPICE PHASE III FLIGHT SAFETY DATA PACKAGE
SPICE-SDP-003

of 230 psid. The gas bottles are DOT bottles with a rated pressure of 500 psi which will be
pressurized to a maximum of 230 psi. To prevent overpressurization this will be verified after
filling that the bottles are the correct mixture and at the correct pressure. The bottles have been
proof tested to 1000 psi. The Experiment Assembly is not sealed and will not pressurize in the
event of full open flow from the mass flow controller. The maximum flow rate of the fuel system
is 20 liters per minute. The fan that is used to cause flow is capable of 1120 liters per minute
with a pressure drop of parts of a psi. Therefore the flow rate through the chamber is orders of
magnitude greater than the maximum fuel flow into the chamber. Therefore the chamber will
have no significant increase in pressure. See hazard Report SPICE U5 for analysis of pressure
due to burning of worst case fuel load in the EA. In the event of power loss the Mass Flow
Controller is normally closed, therefore fuel flow will stop.
Materials will be selected and evaluated for stress corrosion and/or fluid compatibility in
accordance with SSP 30233, Space Station Requirements for Materials and Processes.
Hardware will be built in conformance with approved design drawings and specifications. The
gas bottles will be filled to approved procedures to insure that the proper mixture of gas is
placed in the correct bottle and each bottle is filled to the correct pressure.
The Gas Bottle Assemblies and the pressurized portions of the Experiment Assembly Fuel
Delivery System will be proof pressure tested to 1.5 X MDP and Helium Leak tested for an
acceptable leak rate of 1x10E-4 scc/sec Helium in order to validate structural integrity and
containment by design.

During on orbit operations, procedures will be provided to instruct the crew on proper Gas Bottle
Exchange, Experiment Set-up and Operation to prevent Gas Bottle and Experiment Assembly
Fuel Delivery System leakage.

Reference Unique Hazard Report SPICE-U2. Flammability issues of the fuels are covered
in SPICE-U4 and SPICE U5. Projectile issues are covered in SPICE-U3.


4.3.2 Collision

Calculations show that for the bottle under full pressure, the propulsive force of the worst case,
a break off of a tube, only create 0.6 lbs of force for 0.23 seconds. The force required to break
off the tube is 10 lbs at the worst case moment arm with all factors of safety. If the bottle is
being held such that 10 lbs of force is on the bottle, the 0.6 lb propulsive force can easily be
withstood. Each Gas Bottle Assembly by design incorporates a single fault tolerant design. The
Diaphragm Valve and Quick Disconnect are in series on the Gas Bottle Assembly. Should the
Diaphragm Valve fail the Quick Disconnect will contain the gas. The failure of the Quick
Disconnect is backed up by the containment of the Diaphragm Valve. Both the Diaphragm Valve
and Quick Disconnect would need to fail simultaneously in order for the Gas Bottle Assembly to
become a projectile. In addition there is a cap on the QC which although is not a radial vent cap
it will restrict the flow and disperse it at an angle reducing the force. Even if both failures
occurred the Gas Bottle Tubing at the QD end incorporates a 90 degree bend that would
prevent the Gas Bottle assembly from gaining any significant speed (<1.5m/s), and force
(<0.6lbf) or direction that would present a collision hazard.
Reference Unique Hazard Report SPICE-U3.

4.3.6 Release of Experiment Materials
4.3.6.1 Unburned Fuel Release
JSC toxicologist assessment of the fuels used in SPICE have a toxicity rating of 0 and a
flammability rating of 4, See Attachment A, SPICE Hazardous Material Summary Table (HMST).
Also see table 6 for levels of fuel in the lab module.

A concern was raised at the Phase 0/I review concerning effects on the ECLSS due to leaks of
these fuels. The action item to determine any issues with these gases in the quantities from
SPICE was given. The results of the analysis are that leakage of the total amounts of these
fuels is not an issue for ECLSS. Reference the March 25, 2004 email from Tim Smith which is
attached to hazard report SPICE-U1. Leakage of fuel from SPICE is being controlled by design
and verified by Helium leak testing of the Fuel Delivery System and the bottle assemblies. The
gases that have been analyzed for ECLSS will be used to fill the bottle assemblies by using
approved procedures, by labeling each bottle assembly for contents, and then by sampling each
bottle to test for the correct contents.

Reference Attachment E Unique Hazard Report SPICE-U1.

Flammability in the US Lab. Lean Flammability Limit (LFL) Calculations conducted on the
HMST listed SPICE Fuel Concentrations that could be released inside the United States Lab
(USL) Volume show that all 12 SPICE Gas Bottles could leak their entire contents into the USL
Volume and still be below the LFL for the applicable Fuel. The MSG will be in the Columbus
module at the time SPICE is run. This module has a smaller volume than the US Lab. A new
HMST has been requested and new calculations were performed based on the volume of 64 m3
that is being used by toxicology. See table 6 for the calculations.

Flammability in Stowage. The bottles are launched packed in foam in stowage cases which
are in turn packed in foam. The bottles have all been vibration tested in the stowage
configuration and leak tested. The fittings are all commercial fittings rated for over 3000 psi.
Also each Gas Bottle Assembly incorporates a single fault tolerant design, which would require
the Diaphragm Valve and Quick Disconnect to fail simultaneously in order for the Gas Bottle
Assembly to leak unburned fuel through the normal flow path. This fault tolerance is
accomplished by having the Diaphragm Valve and Quick Disconnect in series on the Gas Bottle
Assembly. Should the Diaphragm Valve fail the Quick Disconnect will contain the gas. The
failure of the Quick Disconnect is backed up by the containment of the Diaphragm Valve. There
will be no ignition sources in the Gas Bottle Assembly stowage cases (no electronics or
batteries). The bottles will be electrically connected to the aluminum case by two redundant
strips of aluminum tape attached to the foam which will contact each bottle. A calculation will be
performed providing the necessary volume in stowage which must be clear of ignition sources
assuming two worst case bottles leak. This is based on being below the LFL. See table 6. The
current calculation with a 75 % packing factor (there is only 25 % void space) is 156 liters or a
54 cm cube. This size is easily accommodated considering the amount of foam used. Once the
fuel leaks into the MPLM or ISS stowage volume it was agreed that it will dissipate rapidly.

Stowage for landing utilizes the same controls, including valve handle restraints. The restraint (a
tie-wrap) is capable of withstanding 50 lbs of force, which is sufficient to prevent turning of the
valve during launch and to prevent inadvertent turning during handling. See figure 10b for a

Flammability in the MSG. This concern is divided into three concerns, fuel build up in the EA
igniting, fuel leak into the MSG igniting and gas leak impinging on a crew member’s hand.

For the concern of the EA filling with fuel and igniting during times with out crew access to the
inside of the EA, the MFC has been leak tested at MDP to demonstrate that it will not leak into
chamber when off, preventing fuel in the chamber from building up or flowing in quantities that
could ignite. Air flow from the EA fans keep the EA internal fuel percentage below the LFL. The
EA fan is on automatically upon power up of SPICE. Also the air flow rate is verified by the crew
from the laptop which has a measured value of air flow rate displayed. The only ignition source
in the EA chamber is the ignitor and the ignitor is not turned on until after the EA fan is verified
on. The crew will turn on the ignitor before fuel flow which will eliminate fuel build up. In the
event that the fan fails, the crew fails to verify the fan speed and there was an ignition source
the total energy content of the EA chamber is well below the 14,240 ft-lb limit for a pressure
vessel at worst case fuel mixture. See table 6.

A flame out condition has been proposed to cause fuel build up if the fuel is not turned off or fuel
is not ignited. For this condition to occur the EA fan would also have to be off. Large amounts
of air in the line could cause flame out. An air bubble caused by changing bottles has been
hypothesized as causing a flame out. The QC from manufacturer is “drip-less” meaning it has
minimal air entrainment (0.3 cc). This air bubble would be pushed out in the first 2 seconds of
operation before ignition was confirmed (before steady state operation), therefore the situation
of a flame out or failure to ignite in 2-3 seconds would not be a surprise situation for the crew, it
would be part of the ignition sequence. Only approved crew procedures will be used to monitor
burn to adjust flame. In addition it has been shown that the air bubble would mix in the fuel line
filter to a percentage of fuel/air that would not be an issue. See attachments to hazard report
SPICE-U5 for analysis.

For the MSG concern, in general Lean Flammability Limit (LFL) Calculations conducted on the
HMST listed SPICE Fuel Concentrations that could be released inside the MSG Working
Volume show that 2 SPICE Gas Bottles could leak their entire contents into the MSG Working
Volume and still be below the LFL for the applicable Fuel, see table 6. Note only one bottle at a
time will be permitted in the MSG. This assumes total mixing which is realistic considering that
there is a minimum of 180 liters/sec of air flowing in the MSG caused by the MSG fans. The
MSG fans are capable of the fuel concentrations below the LFL, and in addition the EA fan will
mix the air in the MSG WV. See attachments to hazard report SPICE-U5 for analysis. There are
no ignition sources inside MSG to ignite any leak from the gas bottles. The switches used for
the SPICE inhibits and ignition will not produce any sparks or electrical ignition sources. SPICE
is designed to minimize the potential for leaks, and with the use of approved crew procedures to
ensure the MSG fans are running and only one bottle is permitted in MSG at a time there should
not be fuel ignition in MSG. See unique hazard SPICE-U8.

In the case of MSG being powered down or power failing there is a concern that a pool of fuel
which leaks out of the Bottle Assembly or EA will ignite when the MSG is powered on. This is
addressed in the attachment to hazard report SPICE-U5 which shows that in the worst case the
fuel will dissipate due to molecular action in less than 28 minutes, but other factors reduce that
time. A slow leak would mean less fuel to dissipate or allow a longer dilution time. A fast leak
would increase mixing due to gas velocity. Since the crew is necessary for the total operation of
SPICE, when the crew leaves the MSG it will be required that the crew turn off the hand valve
reducing the leak potential. In the event of a power loss, when power is restored to the lab,
racks must be individually turned back on, this includes the MSG. If a wait time of ½ hour were
implemented it would insure adequate dilution of the fuel.

In the case of impingement the leak controls mentioned before for the bottles are still valid. In
addition, the pressurized area of the EA will be leak tested to insure that it does not leak. The
Quick Connects (QC) softgood seal resides on the bottle side of the connection which will be
used only once. The EA side which will mate 12 times (once for each bottle) is the metal side,
thus reducing the risk of leakage due to seal wear. Electronics are contained inside the EA. The
only exposed item is a switch which is a 5 volt switch. 5 volt systems are frequently used in
flammable atmospheres (mines) due to their inability to ignite them. In addition this switch is
sealed. The ignitor throw is a lever that the crew moves to insert the ignitor into the fuel path
and turn on the ignitor. It is a lever on a rod which goes inside the EA to move the ignitor. There
is a hall effect sensor (digital) which senses the proximity of the ignitor to the nozzle area and
electronically turns on the ignitor. Therefore there are no sparks associated with turning on the
ignitor. There are also no sparks in the proximity of the crew’s hand to light a fuel impingement.
See figure 16.

2.0 SIGNIFICANT SAFETY DISCUSSION
2.1 SPICE Science and Hardware Overview: SPICE continues to study fundamental phenomena to
understand the mechanisms controlling the stability and extinction of jet diffusion flames. SPICE will
stabilize an enclosed laminar flame, measure the overall flame shape to validate the theoretical and
numerical predictions, measure the flame stabilization heights, and measure the temperature field to
verify flame structure predictions. The objective of the SPICE experiment is to find the laminar
smokepoint properties of nonbuoyant jet diffusion flames (i.e., the properties of the largest laminar jet
diffusion flames that do not emit soot) for several fuels under different nozzle diameter/ co-flow velocity
configurations. Luminous flame shape measurements would also be made to verify models of the flame
shapes under co-flow conditions. The soot point is a simple measurement that has been found useful to
study the influence of flow and fuel properties on the sooting propensity of flames. This information
would help support current understanding of soot processes in laminar flames and by analogy in
turbulent flames of practical interest. Unfortunately, while the laminar smoke-point properties of
buoyant jet diffusion flames at normal gravity (ng) are well known, corresponding information for
nonbuoyant flames is limited and questionable. Thus, the measurements of the laminar smoke-point
properties proposed here would also reduce the risk of future experiments involving hydrocarbon-fueled
laminar jet diffusion flames in spacecraft.
