1.0      INTRODUCTION

1.1      Purpose

This document defines the specific requirements to be satisfied throughout the implementation of the software for
the Smoke Aerosol Measurement Experiment (SAME) instrument. The information contained within this document
provides the technical requirements to be implemented and enables the development team to assess whether or not
the completed software complies with the requirements of the system. The data included in this document provides
the criteria for acceptance of the integrated software configuration.

1.2      Scope

The requirements specified herein apply only to SAME instrument software and custom MSG Laptop Computer
(MLC) software. All ground based telemetry data processing and command uplink software requirements will be
identified in future documents as required.

2.0       INSTRUMENT OVERVIEW

The SAME instrument is designed to quantify the size and distribution of smoke particulate in low gravity so that
this information is available for the future design of effective low gravity smoke detectors. The instrument will also
be used to evaluate the performance of two existing U.S. spacecraft smoke detectors and one commercial smoke
detector. Measurements will be made for the smoke from several materials found in spacecraft considering the
effects of sample temperature, air flow, smoke residence time and aging. The primary targets are materials that
produce liquid smoke particulate but solid smoke producers will also be considered.

The instrument is installed and operated in the MSG Work Volume (WV) which provides a minimal level of
containment (see Figure 1). A general description of the major subsystems follows below. See SAME-DOC-001,
Concept Description Document, for more detail.
      •   Sample Carousel Turning Module, Sample Carousel and Heater Wire Coil
          These components are used to select and heat a sample in order to generate smoke.
      •   Camera
          A camera and light(s) are mounted to see the sample and production of smoke.
      •   Aging Chamber
          The aging chamber has a piston and two gate valves for holding the smoke for a time before releasing it
          downstream. The piston is moved by pressure and vacuum provided by MSG and controlled by SAME.
      •   Thermal Precipitator Module
          This assembly collects samples of smoke on TEM grids for post-flight inspection. It has access to smoke
          before and after the aging chamber.

•   Smoke Diagnostics
    After the aging chamber, the smoke is passed to several diagnostics: the TSI P-Trak™ particle
    concentration counter, the TSI DustTrak™, a residential-grade ionization detector, the ISS photoelectric
    smoke detector and the STS ionization smoke detector.
•   Fluids Control Unit
    Pressure and vacuum supplied by MSG is controlled for smoke aging chamber piston movement and for all
    other fluid flow to and from the diagnostic equipment.
•   System Diagnostics
    Besides the diagnostic instruments measuring smoke characteristics, there are pressure transducers,
    temperature gauges, and voltage and current sensors to monitor system performance.
•   External Interfaces
    External interfaces provided by MSG include power, video recording, temperature control, post-
    pyrolization venting, nitrogen and air supply/vacuum, an RS-422 interface for commanding and telemetry,
    ethernet for file transfer, and a laptop for crew access to the SAME experiment. A SAMS unit measures
    local µg and vibrations and forwards that data to the ground requiring no SAME or MSG processing.
•   Avionics
    Contained in the Data Acquisition Control Unit (DACU) box, the SAME avionics conduct automated
    experiments, controlling the other sub-systems to produce and analyze the smoke. Commands from the
    ground are processed. Experiment data is saved to files on a local storage medium and forwarded to MSG
    for subsequent downlink. Housekeeping data is downlinked every second and supplied to MSG. Off-
    nominal conditions are reported to MSG and the ground. In addition, a means for the crew to access
    SAME data and control is provided.

3.0     AVIONICS OVERVIEW

SAME avionics are located in the DACU within the MSG Work Volume (WV). Cables will stretch to the SAME
diagnostic hardware for control and feedback, to J311 of the WV for command and data transfer, and to J404 of the
WV for ethernet connection to the MSG laptop computer (MLC) for file transfer and crew display operation. There
is also a cable for the camera.

3.1     Data Interfaces

To interface to the MSG, and subsequently to ground systems, the DACU CPU will communicate primarily to the
MSG Rack Controller, also known as the SPLC (Standard Payload Computer) using an RS-422 interface (see Figure
2). SAME will use this interface to receive uplink commands and send health and status (H&S) data and low rate
telemetry (LRT) data.




                            Figure 2          SAME to MSG Data Interfaces

Payload commands are transmitted from the ground through the ISS Payload MDM and eventually to the SPLC via
MIL-STD-1553. The SPLC abstracts this interface from the payload developer, relieving the burden of knowing the
details about the 1553 bus and command validation.

A packet containing SAME health and status is automatically transmitted in the reverse direction on the 1553 bus
and is received on the ground. Also included in the payload health and status packet is the Experiment Control
Word or ECW, a single dedicated memory word that represents the status of the payload. The ECW is monitored by
various ISS subsystems and provides a quick alert if an off-nominal condition is indicated in it.




                                                        4
                                                                                             SAME-DOC-004 Rev D


File-based SAME science data such as experiment measurement data and instrument ancillary data will be
transferred to the MLC using FTP utilizing a 10BaseT ethernet connection. Once these files are stored on the MLC,
the MSG Operations Team at the Payload Operations Integration Center (POIC) will periodically initiate their
downlink and forward them to the SAME Operations Team. Since SAME is not permitted to transmit data on the
ISS LAN, the DACU will automatically switch the active network connection from the MLC to SAME and ISS
using a custom designed ethernet switch when the FTP transfer is required. In its normal state, the MLC will be
connected to ISS LAN.

The camera looking at the SAME sample is hooked up via an NTSC cable to a feedthrough inside the WV which is
then patched on the outside to a video recorder channel in the video drawer.

3.2        DACU Overview

The DACU is the central point for SAME control. It is ultimately responsible for all experiment operations and
interfacing with the environment external to SAME. It contains all of the logic necessary to autonomously operate
experiments. A brief list of its major responsibilities is as follows:
      1.   Receive, validate and process commands from the MSG.
      2.   Periodically transmit SAME system status and select parameters to the MSG.
      3.   Transmit completed and requested data files to the MLC via FTP.
      4.   Manage and store all science and system files on the local data storage medium.
      5.   Autonomously control the progress of preprogrammed tests.
      6.   Provide diagnostic information and manual control of all sensors and controls using the GSE port.
      7.   Control the MSG video recorder(s) capturing SAME images.
      8.   React to sensor(s) out of tolerance by notifying MSG and ground systems, and performing software inhibits
           or other actions as appropriate.

3.3        Crew Display Overview

During long periods of LOS or failure of telemetry, it is beneficial to provide limited visibility into SAME
operation. Payloads may utilize the MLC to present payload status to the crew and provide a mechanism to control
the payload directly from it. Two options for providing the SAME Crew Display are possible. Option 1 is the
preferred solution at this time because of a likely delay in an upgrade to provide a dedicated ethernet link between
the MLC and an experiment.

3.3.1      Option 1 - Custom Win32 or Java Based Application
All data transmitted to the SPLC via H&S and LRT is also transmitted to the MLC via the MSG 1553 bus, and is
therefore available on the MLC. SAME may develop a custom Win32-based or Java-based application that displays
a limited set of the SAME downlink data for the crew to see and provides commanding to the SAME instrument.

3.3.2      Option 2 - Embedded Web Pages

A dedicated ethernet interface between the MLC and SAME may be leveraged to provide a web-based interface into
the DACU using the MLC native web browser. The operating system running on the DACU is capable of running
an embedded web-server such as Apache or NASA’s Tempest servers.

                                      Table 1              Status LEDs

4.3       Data Storage Requirements

The data storage requirements reflect two main needs. First, that a record of all status, measurements and events is
preserved beyond the flight of the instrument. And second, that individual values of status, measurements or events
can be easily located in stored data.

4.3.1     Storage Media
[SAME-SW-REQ-4-3-1-1-1]                  The DACU shall store all files on a media that can preserve all generated
                                         files for extraction after payload down-mass.
[SAME-SW-REQ-4-3-1-1-2]                  The storage media shall have a capacity of at least 520 megabytes.

4.3.2     File Conventions

4.3.2.1      Directory Structure

Because the execution of ISS instrument experiments are highly scheduled, the date and time that procedures and
ground commanding is performed is usually reliably recorded by NASA cadre and experiment ground operation
teams. As such, finding the data files for a previously run experiment or for a particular logged event is facilitated
by a data storage structure based on the date. Therefore,
[SAME-SW-REQ-4-3-2-1-1]                  All files created shall be organized into a hierarchical directory structure
                                         based on the date that files were created.

4.3.2.2      File Names
[SAME-SW-REQ-4-3-2-2-1]                  All files created shall have unique file names.

4.3.3     Stored Data

4.3.3.1      Housekeeping Data
[SAME-SW-REQ-4-3-3-1-1]                  Once per second data relating to the status of the instrument shall be stored
                                         to a file.
[SAME-SW-REQ-4-3-3-1-2]                  Each second of housekeeping data stored to a file shall be time-tagged.

4.3.3.2      Messages
[SAME-SW-REQ-4-3-3-2-1]                  Messages generated by the instrument shall be stored to a file.
[SAME-SW-REQ-4-3-3-2-2]                  Each message stored to a file shall be time-tagged.

4.3.3.3      Experiment Results
[SAME-SW-REQ-4-3-3-3-1]                  Experiment results shall be stored to a file
[SAME-SW-REQ-4-3-3-3-2]                  Experiment results stored to a file shall be time-tagged.
[SAME-SW-REQ-4-3-3-3-3]                  DELETED.

4.4     Command Requirements

Commands are the primary means of initiating SAME instrument operations. Commands to an instrument are
typically formatted into an array of data and inserted into command packets and then transmitted from the ground
operation station or from the crew display on the MSG MLC on-board the ISS. When command packets reach the
DACU the command data within is extracted and interpreted and the appropriate software functions are executed.

The command requirements below deal mainly with the functions required by the instrument and not with how those
functions are implemented or with how the command data is formatted. See SAME-DOC-005, Software Interface
Control Document, for the latter information.

                              heater temperature and duration so as not to ignite the sample.

6.0      OFF-NOMINAL AND CONTINGENCY REQUIREMENTS

6.1      Maintenance Mode Operation

The software will contain provisions to allow experiment operations to continue in the event of the following:
[SAME-SW-REQ-6-1-1-1-1]                  In the event of a loss of the ability to write to the data hard drive, the system
                                         shall continue to accept uplink commands and transmit status data to ensure
                                         safe operation.
[SAME-SW-REQ-6-1-1-1-2]                  In the event of a loss of ethernet communication between the DACU and
                                         the MLC, operations shall continue. Data recorded on the flight avionics
                                         may be manually commanded for retransmission once communication is
                                         regained.

6.2      Caution and Warning

Three discrete bits in the health and status packet are used to indicate to MSG the presence of a payload condition
warranting a warning, caution, or advisory classification and reaction. See MSFC-RQMT-2888, MSG Investigation
Interface Requirements Document, Section 3.11 for details of each level. The specific payload conditions that result
in warning and caution levels are determined by the Payload Safety Review Panel (PSRP) during Phased Safety
Reviews. Automatic safing will be performed if the warning or caution bit is set. The advisory bit can be used to
reflect errors and off-nominal conditions seen only in the low rate telemetry data to the health and status data. If,
due to limited communication coverage or communication error, the health and status data is received while the low
rate telemetry is not, then these off-nominal conditions will still be noted and time-tagged.

6.2.1    Warning

There are no conditions that constitute a “Class 2 -Warning” condition and a subsequent set of the Warning bit in the
Health and Status packet payload ECW.

6.2.2    Caution

There are no conditions that constitute a “Class 3 - Caution” condition and a subsequent set of the Caution bit in the
Health and Status packet payload ECW.

6.2.3    Advisory
There are no conditions that constitute a “Class 4 - Advisory” condition and a subsequent set of the Advisory bit in
the Health and Status packet payload ECW.

6.3      Automatic Safing
[SAME-SW-REQ-6-3-1-1-1]                  In the event of a GN2 regulated pressure reading of over 21 psig, the DACU
                                         shall automatically shut off the main GN2 valve.
[SAME-SW-REQ-6-3-1-1-2]                  In the event of a vacuum pressure reading of over 19 psia, the DACU shall
                                         automatically shut off the main vacuum valve.
